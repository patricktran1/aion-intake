# AION Intake — the product

**Pre-visit intelligence for dermatology.**
*Walk into the room already knowing the story.*

## The wedge

AION Intake interviews a dermatology patient in the days before an in-person
appointment, and hands the dermatologist the history they would otherwise spend
the first third of the visit extracting.

That is the entire product. Not a chart, not a portal, not a visit.

## Why it exists

A traditional intake form asks forty generic questions and produces data no
clinician trusts, so the dermatologist retakes the history anyway. The form cost
the patient ten minutes and saved the physician nothing.

AION Intake asks nine questions at most, and which nine depends on the first
answer. A patient who says "there's a dark spot on my back my wife says has
changed" gets asked about change, bleeding, and sun history. A patient who says
"my hair is falling out in the shower" gets asked about pattern, scalp symptoms,
and what happened three months before it started. Neither is asked the other's
questions.

## Who it is for

**The patient**, once, before a visit they have already booked. Mobile, three to
five minutes, typing or talking.

**The dermatologist**, once, in the thirty seconds before they open the door.

There is no third role. No administrator console, no practice manager, no
enterprise hierarchy.

## The golden path

1. The patient opens a link. They see the practice, the date, roughly how long
   this takes, and one clear statement of what this is and is not.
2. **"What would you most like the dermatologist to help you with?"** They answer
   in their own words.
3. The engine picks a complaint pathway — rash, lesion, acne, hair loss, or a
   sensible general dermatology route — and asks what that pathway needs.
4. Optionally, up to three reference photographs.
5. **"Here's what you told us."** Every line editable.
6. Submit.
7. The dermatologist opens a one-screen brief: headline, timeline, symptoms,
   what has been tried, relevant context, the patient's own goal, the photos,
   and a short list of what is still worth asking in the room.
8. They read or edit the draft HPI, copy it into their record, see the patient,
   type their findings, and generate a draft note.

## The two promises

**To the patient:** *"That was much easier than filling out a form."*
Nine questions instead of forty. Their own words, kept.

**To the dermatologist:** *"I already know what I need to know."*
Thirty seconds of reading replaces five minutes of history-taking.

## What the practice still does

AION Intake replaces the **history-taking** portion of a visit. It does not
replace the front desk. Insurance, demographics, consent forms, pharmacy
preference, and the review of systems are all still the practice's — deliberately.
Duplicating what the medical assistant already collects would make the tool a
second data-entry burden instead of a saving.

## How we know it worked

See `METRICS.md`. The four numbers that matter are: can a patient finish in under
five minutes, can a dermatologist understand the case in under thirty seconds,
does the dermatologist repeat fewer questions in the room, and is the draft HPI
good enough to edit rather than rewrite.
